A high-contrast, black-ink drawing rendered in meticulous cross-hatching. The camera occupies an impossible position: looking vertically upward from the pitch-black void inside an elevator shaft, peering directly through the scratched glass floor of an ascending, fluorescent-lit elevator cab. 

Through the transparent pane, mundane commuter life unfolds in sharp detail—polished linoleum edges, a briefcase, and a commuter’s sensible leather oxfords. Beside the commuter stands the creature, its immense weight pressing flat against the glass. Its lower body is supported by four hyper-segmented legs jointed like folding carpenter’s rulers, snapping backward at jagged, geometry-defying angles. The creature’s towering torso is composed of densely packed, vertically aligned ivory piano keys, bound together by thick, braided embroidery thread. 

Instead of a neck and head, the torso branches into three stiff, curving stalks. Each stalk terminates in an obsidian rotary telephone dial, with glossy human hair spilling out from the numbered finger holes like black grease. The extreme upward angle dramatizes the creature’s impossible height as it stoops slightly to fit within the standard ceiling clearance, its ruler-joints casting spider-like, stark black shadows down through the glass floor into the empty abyss.
