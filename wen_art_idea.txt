Viewed from the floor of the abyss looking directly upward in steep, dizzying perspective, the water column is rendered in dense cross-hatching and stark negative space. Far above, where the sunlit surface should be, sits an infinite, polished parquet hardwood floor, with upside-down dining chairs clinging to it like inverted barnacles. 

Suspended throughout the vast brine are not sea creatures, but an armada of fully inflated, anatomical human lungs. They drift and undulate like translucent manta rays, their vascular networks branching like pale coral. From the bottom of each organ, an elongated trachea hangs straight down, terminating in a heavy cast-iron plumb bob that hovers inches above the viewer's vantage point, tethering the floating respiratory masses to the deep.

Darting between these suspended organs are synchronized schools of pointed fountain-pen nibs, schooling like anchovies. Where their steel tips cut the saline, they discharge thin, razor-sharp ribbons of black ink that spiral upward toward the ceiling. The sea floor itself consists of cracked ceramic bathroom tiles receding into the darkness, punctuated not by sea anemones, but by clusters of meticulously rendered human ears sprouting like limpets from the grout.
