A dramatic worm’s-eye perspective looking straight up from a rain-slicked cobblestone alley flanked by towering, soot-stained industrial tenements. Heavy black-ink linework, dense crosshatching, and stark negative space define the Victorian-era brick facades, tangled telephone wires, and wrought-iron fire escapes that converge steeply toward the zenith. 

The single physically impossible element bisects the composition: the city’s wide, deep river does not stay in its bed, but bends sharply ninety degrees upward at the alley’s end, rising into the sky as a colossal, vertical pillar of churning water. This monolithic, aquatic tower ascends vertically like a liquid skyscraper, perfectly rectangular and held together by unseen tension. Small, iron-hulled tugboats navigate straight up its near-vertical surface, their hulls cleaving churning white wakes that defy gravity, while black plumes of coal smoke rise in tandem with the upward-rushing current. 

The river vanishes into the high overcast, while long, suspended droplets of river spray hang frozen in the air like suspended ink droplets directly above the viewer, catching the harsh white light of the sky against the heavy, inked shadows of the urban canyon below.
