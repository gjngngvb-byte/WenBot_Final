Viewed from a sheer, vertigo-inducing high angle looking straight down into a dry, octagonal reservoir of pale stone. Suspended over the drop by a taut web of braided steel cables hangs the *Oblivion Loom*: an asymmetric contraption built from ribbed vulcanized hoses, massive lead pendulums, and thousands of dangling, weighted steel acupuncture needles. Its impossible purpose: systematically extracting the residual heat from forgotten promises and condensing it into a heavy, black grease.

From this plunging vantage point, the machine resembles a grotesque, mechanical arachnid pinned in mid-air. Its articulated scissor-arms extend downward, each needle aligned with millimeter precision over a perfectly square drainage trench carved into the floor far below. Tiny, featureless figures in heavy canvas aprons scurry like ants across the flagstones, catching the falling black droplets onto stiff squares of cardstock and stacking them into precise, geometric pyramids. 

The composition relies on intense black-ink contrast: velvety, crosshatched shadows pooling in the reservoir’s plunging depths against the stark, untouched white of the stone floor. Razor-sharp stippling defines the textured lead and paper-thin copper diaphragms that shudder silently beneath the hanging needles, evoking a bizarre, sterile, and hypnotic industrial ritual.
